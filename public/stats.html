01Registers

Seven in ten sentences are oral literature — uwepeker, kamuy yukar and other narratives recorded from the last generations of native speakers. The modern registers are small beside that, and they are where Ainu is being written today.

02Dialects

Two Hokkaidō varieties — Saru and Shizunai — supply well over half of the corpus. The Sakhalin record is thin but present, from Asai Take's folktales to Piłsudski-era letters.

Methods & data

  • Every sentence inherits the register of its source collection; the collection→register table is part of this repository.
  • Word-level statistics count Latin-script tokens; forms are compared accent-insensitively and displayed in their commonest spelling.
  • Keyness: log-likelihood G² (Dunning 1993), each register against the rest of the corpus, with rates per million tokens. Words need 20 corpus occurrences and 5 in the register to qualify; single-letter tokens are excluded, since speaker labels and list markers dominate them.
  • Function words are flagged from the tagger: closed-class UD tags and clitics.
  • N-grams follow the token layer: consecutive tokens inside one sentence, clitics counted as tokens.
  • Sentence length counts word tokens (Latin and kana script); histogram bins of two, overflow at 31+.
  • POS tags: Universal Dependencies, machine-tagged; of tokens carry a tag.
  • Zipf series truncated at rank 1,000.
  • Snapshot · data: stats.json · every chart is inline SVG/CSS drawn at load.
